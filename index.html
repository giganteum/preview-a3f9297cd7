How it adapts to your site

A model trained on one forest usually struggles on the next, and on different imagery or a different season. We take a detector from open data down to your domain: your sensor, your forest, your season. Three stages.

Stage 1Our own base models

Trained in-house on crown annotations from around the world, across resolutions, so one model handles drone, aerial and satellite imagery.

Starting point for everything below.
Stage 2Aligned to your domain

We correct the model's output on a sample of your own imagery and train on those corrections. Correcting is much faster than labeling from scratch, so this takes days rather than months.

We measure the review effort needed on your imagery.
Stage 3Frozen, measured, improved on your terms

We freeze and version the model so results remain reproducible. We check each new capture on a reviewed sample before relying on its predictions. When an update helps, we test and release a new version.

Each capture is checked on a reviewed sample.

Every step is scored on imagery held back from training, and we show you the numbers. It works with the imagery you already have, or we help you get it: drone flights, public aerial archives with years of history, or 30 cm commercial satellite imagery when you need to cover a lot of ground.

Aerial wildlife surveys

Most survey frames are empty. We find the ones that aren't.

A survey flight produces tens of thousands of frames, and the bulk of the work is looking at nothing. Our pipeline discards the empty frames, finds the animals in the rest, and sorts them by species.

It is built for surveys that change: new regions, seasons, aircraft and camera setups it has never seen, without retraining from scratch. Before Giganteum, our team built and ran this kind of pipeline on national-scale surveys.

Beyond trees

The same team, pointed at your problem.

Trees are the mission. When a project fits what we know and helps fund that mission, we take it on. What we know is building models that keep working after they leave the lab: in new places, new seasons, and on imagery nobody trained them for.

Before Giganteum, our team spent twenty years at this: identifying individual animals in photographs from camera traps, dive cameras, drones and survey aircraft; counting wildlife across whole landscapes from the air; mapping habitat from satellite; and building the datasets, pipelines and agentic research tooling behind it. That work is in daily use by thousands of researchers across more than a hundred species.

What that experience covers:

  • ML systems that survive deployment. Inference, quality checks, monitoring and validation for models in daily use.
  • Detection, classification and individual re-identification from overhead and ground imagery.
  • Large scientific datasets. Curation, provenance, security and the tooling to review them.
  • Feasibility, proposals and program strategy for conservation and remote-sensing work.

Who we are

Giganteum is a small nonprofit with a startup's metabolism: a small team, no bureaucracy, twenty years of conservation technology behind us and trees ahead.

Those years were spent building the whole stack: new algorithms and the research behind them, data gathered from every continent, and models for detection, classification and re-identification that run in the field every day. We're bringing that experience to individual-tree monitoring.
